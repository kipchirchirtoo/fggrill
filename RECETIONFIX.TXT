## Current DB and Codebase Analysis — Kyogong Shared Reception Drawer

I reviewed the uploaded backend, Flutter and live-database analyses. The main conclusion is:

> **Your current architecture can support one receptionist handling both Reception and Restaurant using one cash drawer, but the hotel-payment flow is currently split into two settlement paths. One path enters the cashier shift correctly; the other can collect money without entering shift reconciliation.**

That is likely the main reason figures such as **Today Revenue**, **Shift Collections** and **Expected Drawer Cash** can disagree.

## 1. What is already correct

### Reception already embeds the Cashier module

The Reception shell contains:

* Reception operations
* Room Bills
* Embedded Cashier Station
* POS Terminal
* Petty Cash
* Shift management

Reception’s normal Room Bills screen is read-only. Selecting **Pay at Cashier** passes the hotel confirmation number to the embedded Cashier Station, which then resolves the guest and folio. 

This means you do **not** need:

* A separate Reception Cashier application
* A separate Reception cash drawer
* Another cashier shift table
* Another payment architecture

The same employee can operate the two functions from one login.

---

## 2. Correct cashier source of truth

The live database confirms:

* `cashier_shifts` has **0 rows** and is legacy.
* `cashier_shift_logs` has **173 rows** and is the real cashier shift lifecycle.
* `cashier_transactions` has **18,493 rows**.
* `shift_transactions` has **18,168 rows**.
* `cashier_logbooks` and `cashier_logbook_lines` feed reconciliation and auditing. 

Therefore, Kyogong’s shared drawer must use:

```text
cashier_shift_logs
        │
        ├── Reception room payments
        ├── Restaurant payments
        ├── Booking deposits
        ├── Conference collections
        ├── Petty-cash payouts
        ├── Cash refunds
        └── Other cashier-controlled money
```

Do not revive or rebuild around `cashier_shifts`.

The master record should remain:

> **One active `cashier_shift_logs` row for Mercy’s physical drawer.**

Restaurant orders can remain connected to their existing `pos_outlet_shifts`; those outlet shifts represent operational sales and stock accountability, not separate physical drawers.

---

## 3. The critical problem: two hotel-payment paths

### Path A: Cashier Station using the HTL reference

This is the safer path.

When the cashier searches an `HTL…` confirmation number:

1. `getBillDetails` finds the reservation and folio.
2. It calculates accommodation and charge-to-room items.
3. `processPayment` inserts a row into `payments`.
4. The payment is linked to the active cashier shift.
5. The reservation payment status is updated.
6. The folio is also settled.

This route correctly understands that the receptionist physically received money during an active cashier shift. 

### Path B: Direct Room Bills settlement

The Cashier version of `RoomBillsView` can call:

```http
POST /room-charge/folio/:reservationId/settle
```

The endpoint inserts only a folio `transactions` payment row. The folio balance falls, but the uploaded codebase audit identifies that this payment is **not yet posted into the cashier’s shift collection and reconciliation ledger**. 

Therefore, the current system can produce this situation:

```text
Guest pays KES 10,000 cash
        │
        ├── Folio balance reduces by KES 10,000       ✓
        ├── Guest appears settled                     ✓
        ├── Physical drawer contains KES 10,000       ✓
        ├── Shift Collections increases               ✗
        └── Expected Drawer Cash increases            ✗
```

This is the most important defect for the Kyogong setup.

## 4. Why your screenshot can show conflicting figures

Your dashboard showed revenue while the Cashier Station showed:

* Shift Collections: KES 0
* Expected Drawer Cash: KES 0

The documents do not prove which exact transaction produced that screenshot, but the direct Room Bills settlement gap is a strong possible cause:

* Payment may have settled the guest folio.
* Revenue may therefore appear on Reception.
* No matching `cashier_transactions` or shift collection line may have been generated.

Other possible causes are that the payment was recorded outside Mercy’s currently active shift or under a different cashier shift. The database should be checked for the underlying transaction before correcting historical values.

## 5. Existing tables already support the solution

`cashier_transactions` already contains the fields needed to differentiate money without creating additional tables:

* `cashier_shift_log_id`
* `cashier_id`
* `source_module`
* `source_document_type`
* `source_document_id`
* `source_document_number`
* `revenue_type`
* `confirmation_number`
* `payment_method`
* `amount`
* `amount_tendered`
* `change_given`
* `payment_reference`
* `customer_name`

The table was designed to record one physical cashier’s transactions while retaining the original source and revenue category. 

A room payment should therefore look conceptually like:

```text
cashier_shift_log_id = Mercy's open shift
source_module        = RECEPTION
source_document_type = ROOM_FOLIO
source_document_id   = reservation/folio ID
revenue_type         = ROOM_BOOKING
payment_method       = CASH
amount               = KES 10,000
confirmation_number  = HTL...
```

A restaurant payment made into the same drawer should look like:

```text
cashier_shift_log_id = Mercy's same open shift
source_module        = POS_RESTAURANT
source_document_type = POS_ORDER
source_document_id   = pos_shift_orders.id
revenue_type         = RESTAURANT
payment_method       = CASH
amount               = KES 2,500
```

The physical drawer is combined, but the revenue remains separately attributable.

## 6. Required code correction

Both hotel-payment buttons must use one common backend payment service.

Recommended internal service:

```text
recordCashierControlledPayment()
```

It should be called by:

* Cashier Station `processPayment`
* Room Bills `settleRoomBill`
* Check-in deposit collection
* Reservation advance deposit
* Checkout payment
* Conference or catering payment received by the receptionist

The complete operation should happen in one database transaction:

1. Confirm the employee has an active `cashier_shift_logs` shift.
2. Lock or validate the active shift.
3. Insert the official `payments` record.
4. Insert the `cashier_transactions` record.
5. Insert the appropriate `shift_transactions` or logbook line.
6. Insert the folio payment into `transactions`.
7. Update `reservations.amount_paid` and `payment_status`.
8. Recalculate the folio.
9. Update the cashier shift payment-method totals.
10. Commit everything together.

If any step fails, the entire payment should roll back.

Do not let the folio settle successfully while the cashier ledger fails.

---

## 7. Shared-drawer calculation

For Mercy’s one drawer:

```text
Expected physical cash
=
Opening float
+ Reception cash received
+ Restaurant cash received
+ Booking deposits received in cash
+ Conference/catering cash received
+ Other cash received
− Petty cash issued
− Cash refunds
− Cash drops/deposits
```

M-Pesa, card, credit bills and room charges must be shown in reconciliation but excluded from physical expected cash.

The existing `cashier_shift_logs` table already has fields for opening float, cash sales, M-Pesa, card, room booking revenue, restaurant revenue, expenses, actual counted cash and variance. 

---

## 8. Charge-to-room must remain non-cash

The current Charge-to-Room flow is conceptually correct:

1. Confirm the branch and outlet feature flags.
2. Validate an active checked-in guest.
3. Post the restaurant bill into the folio.
4. Categorize it as food, beverage or other.
5. Mark the originating POS order as paid using `ROOM_CHARGE`.
6. Do not receive physical money at that point. 

Therefore:

```text
Restaurant revenue attribution     = Yes
Guest folio charge                 = Yes
Cashier physical cash              = No
M-Pesa/card collection             = No
Expected drawer cash movement      = No
```

The restaurant sale remains attributed to Restaurant, while Reception eventually collects the guest’s combined balance.

---

## 9. Another major risk: current checkout behaviour

One backend reference reports that the current check-out code can:

* Set the reservation to `checked_out`
* Set `payment_status = paid`
* Set `amount_paid = total`
* Set the folio balance to zero
* Mark the folio settled

This appears to happen automatically as part of checkout rather than only after proving that payment or approved credit exists. 

That is dangerous because a receptionist could check out an unpaid guest and the system could erase the visible outstanding balance.

Checkout should instead be blocked when:

```text
folio balance > 0
```

unless the remaining balance has been formally converted to:

* Approved credit
* Approved complimentary stay
* Authorised write-off

The system must never treat “checkout completed” as proof that money was received.

---

## 10. Folio consistency problem

The folio system currently has:

* `transactions` as the financial ledger
* `folio_transactions` as the detailed audit ledger
* `room_charges` stored directly on the folio
* `balance_due` and `balance` as mirrored columns

The database trigger calculates charges from `transactions`, but accommodation charges are not stored there as normal charge rows. Application code then has to rewrite totals after the trigger runs. A raw payment or charge insert can therefore temporarily or permanently calculate the wrong balance unless the application-level recomputation also runs. 

For the immediate Kyogong drawer issue, do not redesign the entire folio system. Ensure the common payment service uses the same existing folio recomputation logic after every payment.

A future clean-up should make one ledger authoritative for all accommodation, food, beverage, other charges and payments.

---

## 11. Exact target architecture

```text
MERCY'S MASTER CASHIER SHIFT
cashier_shift_logs
│
├── Restaurant payment
│   ├── pos_shift_orders / pos_master_bills
│   ├── cashier_transactions
│   ├── shift_transactions
│   └── revenue_type = RESTAURANT
│
├── Room payment
│   ├── reservations / folios
│   ├── payments
│   ├── transactions
│   ├── cashier_transactions
│   ├── shift_transactions
│   └── revenue_type = ROOM_BOOKING
│
├── Charge to room
│   ├── POS bill marked ROOM_CHARGE
│   ├── folio charge
│   ├── restaurant revenue retained
│   └── no drawer movement
│
└── Petty cash
    ├── cashier-controlled cash-out
    ├── shift expense line
    └── reduces expected physical cash
```

## Final conclusion

The correct solution is **not to combine Reception and Restaurant accounting into one revenue ledger**.

It is:

> **One `cashier_shift_logs` master shift for the one physical drawer, with every payment linked to that shift and tagged by its original source and revenue type.**

The immediate code priorities are:

1. Route `settleRoomBill` through the same shift-aware payment service as `processPayment`.
2. Block all money collection when there is no active master cashier shift.
3. Prevent checkout from automatically marking unpaid balances as paid.
4. Keep Charge-to-Room as a non-cash folio posting.
5. Derive shift totals from the detailed cashier ledger instead of trusting separately updated summary figures.
6. Reconcile historical folio payments that have no matching cashier-shift transaction.

Until the direct Room Bills route is corrected, Kyogong should settle accommodation bills through the **Cashier Station using the HTL lookup**, because that is the documented path that links the payment to the active cashier shift.
